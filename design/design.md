# Pokemon Yellow: Live Battle

## The game in one breath
You are Red, from Pallet Town. Oak's last Pokemon is a Pikachu that hates its Poke Ball, so it walks at your heels. Together you walk the Kanto of Game Boy Pokemon Yellow, rebuilt in 3D to look like an episode of the first anime season: cel-shaded towns, painted skies, and Pidgey you can see moving in the grass. When a Pidgey bursts out, the screen never cuts away. A gust flattens the grass into a ring and the world beyond it dims. The camera rises and the Route 1 tune picks up drums and fiddle. You steer your Pokemon yourself. You dodge the Gust you saw coming, and Thundershock fires the instant you press it. Then the colour comes back and you walk on. It is Yellow's journey, fought live.

**Genre:** open-world action RPG.

**Pillars**
1. **Battles you fight, not menus you pick.** Timing, positioning and good calls decide every fight.
2. **Pikachu is your partner, not a party slot.** It is always outside, always reacting and always first into battle.
3. **Kanto worth wandering.** Every route has its Yellow Pokemon, trainers and hidden items to find.

## The Yellow rule
After playing the first build, the designer set one rule above all the others: **the story, Pokemon, trainers, routes, towns, items and their order follow Game Boy Pokemon Yellow exactly.** Wild species and levels on each route, trainer teams, move sets, types, stats, prices and dialogue all come from Yellow. The studio adds only how things look, sound and fight. Nothing comes from the TV show's plots and nothing is invented: there are no gym tricks, no named Bug Catchers and no new roadblocks. Where a live fight needs a rule Yellow never had, such as cooldowns or dodges, the studio builds that rule from Yellow's own numbers.

## Look
It should feel like walking through the anime. Everything is cel-shaded in two flat tones with bold ink outlines. Skies are painted backdrops with towering summer clouds. Routes are lush and saturated, full of flowers, round trees and sunlit meadows. Towns are proper towns: Pallet's two houses and Oak's white lab, Pokemon Centres with red roofs, Marts with blue roofs, and gyms with the League emblem over the door. Pikachu's yellow and Red's cap are the brightest things on screen.

Battles use anime effects. Thundershock is a burst of jagged yellow bolts with white cores. Big hits get an impact frame with speed lines and flat graphic sparks. Yellow's battle messages appear as bold captions, such as "It's super effective!" and "It doesn't affect Geodude…", and HP bars drain instead of numbers floating over the fight. Pokemon are drawn larger than life so they read well in the ring.

## World
Kanto is one continuous land with no loading screens, and doors open straight into rooms. The slice runs north: **Pallet Town**, then **Route 1** (a grassy slope with one-way ledges), then **Viridian City**. From there **Route 22** heads west toward the League gate and **Route 2** runs north into **Viridian Forest**, with its dim green light under huge trunks. It ends at **Pewter City**, a grey stone town with the Museum of Science and **Pewter Gym**.

## Roadblocks
These are Yellow's own roadblocks. Each is a person with a line, never an invisible wall.
- **Route 1.** Oak stops Red at the edge of the grass.
- **Viridian Mart.** The clerk hands Red Oak's Parcel and won't sell him anything until it has been delivered.
- **Viridian.** An old man lies across the north road: "You can't go through here! This is private property!" His granddaughter says he hasn't had his coffee yet. Once Red has the Pokedex, the old man is up and shows him how to catch a Pokemon.
- **Pewter.** A boy at the east exit marches Red to the gym until Red holds the Boulder Badge.
- **Later:** the badge guards on Route 23, the thirsty Saffron guards and the Snorlax on Routes 12 and 16.

## The ring
When you touch a wild Pokemon or a trainer reaches you, the camera sweeps low and a gust flattens the ground into a **battle ring** 16–28 metres across. The camera rises and everything outside the ring fades to a washed-out painting. There is never a cut. The ground stays in the fight: a Route 1 ledge splits the ring into high and low ground, and forest trunks become pillars. If there isn't room, the ring slides to open ground. Wild rings have one worn gap in the edge, and standing in it for 3 seconds runs from the fight. Trainer rings are closed all the way round. The lab and the gyms need no ring, because their floor is already the stage.

## The battle camera
The camera sits high behind Red's side and looks down at about 55 degrees. The ring fills four-fifths of the screen, with both Pokemon, Red and every ground shadow in view. The designer chose readability over closeness, because in a live fight you win by reading what is coming. The camera never spins, and leaves in the way fade to outlines. In Pewter Gym it pulls higher so that all of Onix fits in the frame.

## Battles
**You steer your active Pokemon directly.** Moves fire the moment you press them, and every dodge is your own reflex. Red stands at the ring's edge, throws Potions and Poke Balls, and shouts Yellow's lines.

**Four moves, four buttons.** Each move comes from the Pokemon's Yellow move set and has a cooldown:
- **Quick moves** (Thundershock, Tackle, Gust, Scratch) recharge in about 1 second.
- **Tactical moves** (Growl, Tail Whip, Sand-Attack, String Shot, Harden) take 3–8 seconds.
- **Big moves** (Thunder Wave, Bide, Double Kick, Confusion) take 12–20 seconds.

Damage, types, stat changes and status effects follow Yellow's rules. Stat drops last until the fight ends or the Pokemon is swapped out. **Dodge:** every enemy attack warns you first with a crouch, a glow or a ground shadow. **Swap:** calling in another Pokemon starts a 30-second cooldown, and the incoming Pokemon lands with a free dodge. If a Pokemon faints, you must swap even while the cooldown runs. **Healing:** a Potion heals Yellow's 20 HP over 2 seconds. A hit stops the heal, and Potions have a 10-second cooldown.

## Bodies
A Pokemon's body shapes how it fights, so a swap brings a new body as well as a new type.
- **Runners** (Pikachu, Eevee, Rattata, Nidoran, Mankey, Sandshrew) are fast and dodge with a quarter-second dash. They can hop down ledges but not climb back up.
- **Fliers** (Pidgey, Spearow, Butterfree) hover, cross ledges both ways and dodge sideways twice as far. A Rock move knocks them to the ground for a second.
- **Crawlers** (Caterpie, Weedle, Metapod, Kakuna, Geodude, Diglett) are slow and low, so wide sweeps pass over them. They dodge by tucking in place.
- **Giants** (Onix) turn slowly, never dodge and attack in wide sweeps.
- **Swimmers** arrive with Misty and **floaters** arrive at Lavender Tower.

## Catching, encounters and trainers
Wild Pokemon are visible, and each route's species and levels come from Yellow's tables. Pidgey scatter when Red runs near them, Rattata charge and Caterpie cling to trunks. Creeping up while crouched starts the fight with the foe stunned for 2 seconds. To catch a Pokemon, weaken it and throw a ball. The catch meter turns from red to yellow to green, the ball wobbles, and a break-out loses the ball. Red carries six Pokemon, and extras go to Bill's storage.

When a trainer sees Red, an exclamation mark pops up and they walk over. They say their Yellow line, use their Yellow team and send out their next Pokemon when one faints. They pay Yellow's prize money and never challenge him again.

## Blackout
When Red's last Pokemon faints, the caption reads "Red is out of usable Pokemon! Red blacked out!" The world fades to grey. Red wakes at the last Pokemon Centre he used, or at home if he hasn't used one. His party is healed, half his money is gone and Pikachu is worried at his side. Losing the lab battle to Gary carries on without a blackout, as in Yellow.

## Pikachu
Oak's Pikachu refuses its ball, follows Red everywhere and refuses the Thunder Stone. Turn to Pikachu and talk to it to see its face, just as in Yellow. Its friendship rises with battles and level-ups and falls when Pikachu faints.
- **Close:** once per battle, Pikachu dodges an attack on its own.
- **Best friends:** Thundershock recharges a third faster.

## Growth
Levels, learned moves and evolutions follow Yellow. Pikachu starts at level 5 with Thundershock and Growl, learns Tail Whip at level 6 and Thunder Wave at level 8. Learning a fifth move means forgetting one, as in Yellow. Evolution can change a body: Caterpie crawls, Metapod hardens in place and Butterfree flies. Badges give Yellow's stat boosts and set how well traded Pokemon obey.

## Music and sound
**A folk road band** plays Yellow's own tunes on guitar, tin whistle and accordion. Each route theme has a battle layer in the same key and tempo. As a ring forms, drums and fiddle join the tune already playing, and they drop away when the colour returns. The rival and the gyms bring the whole band in at full tilt.

## Story
The story follows Yellow beat for beat:
1. Oak stops Red at the grass and catches a wild Pikachu.
2. Gary grabs the Eevee, Red gets the Pikachu, and Gary challenges him in the lab.
3. Outside, Pikachu won't stay in its ball.
4. Red carries Oak's Parcel back from Viridian and receives the Pokedex.
5. The old man, now awake, shows Red how to catch.
6. Gary waits on Route 22.
7. Bug Catchers ambush Red in Viridian Forest.
8. Red visits the Pewter Museum, then takes on Brock.

Beyond the slice come Jessie and James at Mt. Moon, Misty, the S.S. Anne, the Rocket Hideout, Lavender Tower's ghost, Silph Co., Giovanni and Gary as Champion.

**Brock is the slice's wall.** His Geodude and Onix are Rock and Ground, so Pikachu's Electric moves do nothing to them, and the fight rewards the team Red has built. A Jr. Trainer with Diglett and Sandshrew comes first. Then Geodude curls up and tackles. Onix sweeps its tail, lets out a ring-shaped Screech and uses Bide: it glows orange for 3 seconds, then releases double the damage it took as a shockwave. Brock hands over the Boulder Badge and TM34 Bide.

## Designer's calls
- The ring sweep and the camera's rise take 1 second together, and colour outside the ring drops to 40%.
- Pokemon under 1 metre appear at one and a half times their Pokedex height, with a 45 cm minimum. The cast heights are these shown heights. Giants appear at their true size.
- Runners move at 7 m/s, fliers at 5.5 and crawlers at 3.5.
- Battle captions replace floating damage numbers.
- Onix's Tackle is a tail sweep and its Screech is a ring-shaped wave.
- Pokemon that Yellow only offered through trading come from in-game traders.
- The first slice's art allowance rises because every model is being remade in the anime style and the slice now carries Yellow's full early roster.
- All numbers are provisional until playtest.

## Scope
**Full game:** Yellow's whole journey in about 30 hours. It covers all of Kanto, eight gyms, the Elite Four, Gary as Champion, all 151 Pokemon, Cut, Surf, Strength and Fly, and Cerulean Cave after the Hall of Fame.

**First playable slice:** Pallet Town to the Boulder Badge in about 40 minutes. It includes Yellow's opening, the Parcel errand, Gary twice, the forest Bug Catchers and Brock's two Rock Pokemon, all without a single cut.
